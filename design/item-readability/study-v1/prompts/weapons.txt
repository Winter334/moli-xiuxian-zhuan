Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: painted inventory icon atlas for a Chinese cultivation RPG.
Primary request: redesign exactly four swords together in a strict 2-column by 2-row grid. They must clearly communicate two material tiers and two craft grades at tiny 28-40 pixel icon sizes. Not four recolors of one model.
Subjects, reading order:
Top left, rough iron sword: short narrow straight double-edged blade, plain blunt-looking blade shoulders, tiny straight bar guard and coarse cord-wrapped grip. The blade has broad dull uneven grey forging planes, not tiny scratches. The crudest and simplest complete sword, still usable and unbroken. Compact stocky length-to-grip proportions.
Top right, finished iron sword: long slender gently tapered diamond-ridge blade, clearly curved arched crossguard, neat wrapped grip and modest round pommel. One broad clean bright silver blade plane and crisp dark ridge give it a deliberately worked finish. Taller and slimmer outline than the rough iron sword, but plainly inexpensive iron rather than gilded treasure.
Bottom left, rough dark-steel sword: broad leaf-shaped near-black double-edged blade, visibly thick blade shoulders and a short solid block guard, plain practical grip. The wide heavy blade and large smoky dark forging patches show stronger stock but coarse quenching. NOT the narrow iron blade with a color change. Full sword, not a broken blade.
Bottom right, finished dark-steel sword: wide angular shoulders leading into a smoothly tapering dark blade, a substantial stepped twin-wing crossguard and a fitted metal grip collar. Broad clean cool-silver bevel bands frame one continuous blue-black blade core. The guard is a strong forked-wing silhouette readable at 28-40 px, not tiny decoration. The most deliberately crafted of these four ordinary early swords, still practical and unenchanted.
Family grammar: iron uses slender simple silver blade masses; dark steel uses broader darker structural blade masses. Coarse craft uses small plain block/bar fittings and broad unfinished planes; fine craft uses visibly shaped guards, continuous bevels and fitted collars. BOTH material and craft differences must survive shrinking. Do not rely only on handle color, polish or microscopic engraving.
Composition/framing: each complete sword centered and occupying about 80 percent of an equal square cell, the same diagonal from lower left tip to upper right pommel. Maintain generous pure-white margins and a broad white cross gutter. No tip, guard wing or pommel crosses a cell boundary. All four must have comparable display presence; the crude one is not a tiny distant object.
Style/medium: bright hand-painted game assets, strong contour, broad confident planes, coherent simplified reflections. Enough nonphotographic painting to read as designed game icons, not four photographed museum swords.
Constraints: pure white background for local removal; no ground, cast shadow, frame, label, text, badge, number, watermark or UI. No reference images. No magic effects, ornate gold, serrated fantasy blades, missing hilts or changes of weapon class. Differences are object design, not alternate item quantities.
